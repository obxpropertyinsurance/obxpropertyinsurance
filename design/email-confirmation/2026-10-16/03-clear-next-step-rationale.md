# Your request, clearly

Review concept only. Fictional Jamie, email-first request, coverage needed October 21, 2026. Nothing is sent and no production template is changed.

**Subject:** Jamie, we have your OBX insurance request

**Alternative subjects:** Your OBX insurance request is here · We received your home insurance request

**Preheader:** Your timing, your questions, and your email preference are together for human review.

**Purpose:** Reassure a homebuyer that their request arrived, demonstrate that we paid attention to their timing and contact choice, and make a helpful reply easy.

The visual idea is a small, beautifully typeset working brief. A generous Fraunces headline and Piper give it the site's personality; the three-row recap supplies practical value immediately. There are no progress stamps, claims of completed review, decorative uppercase labels, or invented service guarantees.

The sole primary action is **Add a note by email**, addressed to hello@obxncinsurance.com. In an actual email the recipient can also use Reply. It never asks the visitor to repeat a quote funnel. The body makes one light preparation suggestion: keep lender requirements handy. It deliberately avoids requesting sensitive documents in email.

## Audience and conversion reasoning

- Large 19px body copy, short paragraphs, clear table labels and a 60px-ish button support older readers and mobile use.
- The visible receipt and summary relieve uncertainty before any call to action.
- The reply is optional. A successful outcome can be simple reassurance, a corrected date, or a useful clarification; a click is not required to consider the email successful.
- The email-first statement is for eligible email-first submissions only. Do not send this version to call/text requests, conflicting instructions, contact holds, or historical leads.
- First name, approved request category, date and contact choice are the only example personalization. No address, full name, notes, contact details or lead identifiers appear.

## Tradeoffs and team debate

This is the easiest concept to scan and verify; it is slightly longer and less emotionally coastal than the painting-led option. Keep the recap to three rows, because a more detailed summary would become a form or status dashboard. A human portrait was intentionally omitted here so the message cannot imply that a particular agent has already been assigned.

The strongest candidate for the older audience is this structure, potentially with the postcard's restrained painting added below the receipt. The personal-letter concept may be strongest if replies are the primary outcome, because it can feel more like an individual conversation. These are design judgments, not tested performance claims.

## Production considerations

- Table layout and essential styling are inline. Fraunces is progressive enhancement; Georgia is the email-safe fallback. The browser prototype is not proof that all inboxes support the custom font.
- Replace relative asset references with approved absolute HTTPS URLs or embedded production attachments after selection. Existing local assets are referenced; no copies or new raster assets were generated.
- The footer uses the existing `/privacy-policy/` route verified in the source. Its full text is a receipt disclosure, not a legal certification.
- This concept has no tracking pixels, scripts or click redirects. Do not add open/click tracking as part of design selection.
- Test Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail, dark mode, images disabled, text enlargement and narrow screens before activation. Plain text should retain the same receipt, recap, preparation suggestion, reply invitation and coverage disclaimer.
